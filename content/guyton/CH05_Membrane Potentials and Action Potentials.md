## SKELETAL MUSCLE

### Muscle Fiber Structure

Skeletal muscles are composed of numerous fibers ranging from **10 to 80 micrometers in diameter**. Each fiber extends the entire length of the muscle.

Each fiber is made up of successively smaller subunits. In most skeletal muscles, each fiber is usually innervated by only one nerve ending, located near the middle of the fiber. About 2% of fibers have multiple nerve endings.

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Sarcolemma | True cell membrane (plasma membrane) plus outer polysaccharide coat with collagen fibrils |
| Myofibrils | Several hundred to several thousand per fiber |
| Tendon fibers | Connect muscle to bone |

**Sarcolemma** is the thin membrane enclosing each muscle fiber. It consists of:

- A true cell membrane (plasma membrane)
- An outer coat of thin polysaccharide material containing numerous thin collagen fibrils

At each end of the muscle fiber, the sarcolemma fuses with a tendon fiber. These bundle into muscle tendons that connect muscles to bones.

### Myofibril Composition

Each muscle fiber contains **several hundred to several thousand myofibrils**. Each myofibril is composed of approximately:

| Filament Type | Number per Myofibril | Description |
|---------------|---------------------|-------------|
| Myosin filaments | ~1500 | Thick filaments |
| Actin filaments | ~3000 | Thin filaments |

These filaments are large polymerized protein molecules responsible for muscle contraction. The thick filaments are myosin, and the thin filaments are actin.

### Sarcomere Organization

**Filament Arrangement**

Myosin and actin filaments partially interdigitate, causing myofibrils to have alternating light and dark bands.

| Band Type | Composition | Properties |
|-----------|-------------|------------|
| I bands (light) | Actin filaments only | Isotropic to polarized light |
| A bands (dark) | Myosin filaments + overlapped actin filaments | Anisotropic to polarized light |

**Cross-bridges** are small projections from the sides of myosin filaments. The interaction between these cross-bridges and actin filaments causes contraction.

**Z Disk Structure**

The actin filaments attach at their ends to a Z disk. From the Z disk, these filaments extend in both directions to interdigitate with myosin filaments.

The Z disk is composed of filamentous proteins different from actin and myosin filaments. It passes crosswise across the myofibril and also crosswise from myofibril to myofibril, attaching myofibrils to one another all the way across the muscle fiber. Therefore, the entire muscle fiber has light and dark bands, as do the individual myofibrils. These bands give skeletal and cardiac muscle their striated appearance.

**Sarcomere Definition**

The portion of the myofibril (or of the whole muscle fiber) that lies between two successive Z disks is called a **sarcomere**. When the muscle fiber is contracted, the length of the sarcomere is about **2 micrometers**. At this length, the actin filaments completely overlap the myosin filaments, and the tips of the actin filaments are just beginning to overlap one another. At this length, the muscle is capable of generating its greatest force of contraction.

### Titin Filament Organization

The side-by-side relationship between myosin and actin filaments is maintained by a large number of filamentous molecules of a protein called **titin**.

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Molecular weight | ~3 million Daltons |
| Classification | One of the largest protein molecules in the body |
| Characteristics | Filamentous and very springy |

These springy titin molecules act as a framework that holds the myosin and actin filaments in place so that the contractile machinery maintains proper alignment.
