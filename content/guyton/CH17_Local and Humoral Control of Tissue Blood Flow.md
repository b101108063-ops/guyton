## OTHER CHEMICAL FACTORS

Many different ions and other chemical factors can dilate or constrict local blood vessels. The following list details their specific effects:

| Factor | Effect | Mechanism |
|--------|--------|----------|
| **Calcium ions (Ca²⁺)** | Vasoconstriction | Increase in intracellular calcium stimulates smooth muscle contraction |
| **Potassium ions (K⁺)** | Vasodilation | Inhibits smooth muscle contraction (within physiological range) |
| **Magnesium ions (Mg²⁺)** | Powerful vasodilation | Inhibits smooth muscle contraction |
| **Hydrogen ions (H⁺)** | Vasodilation (↑ H⁺); Vasoconstriction (↓ H⁺) | pH affects arteriolar tone |
| **Acetate and citrate** | Mild vasodilation | Anions with significant vascular effects |
| **Carbon dioxide (CO₂)** | Moderate vasodilation (most tissues); Marked vasodilation (brain) | Direct effect on vessels; indirect effect via sympathetic nervous system |

### Carbon Dioxide and Sympathetic Vasoconstriction

Carbon dioxide in the blood has two effects:

1. **Direct effect**: Causes vasodilation in most tissues — moderate — but marked vasodilation in the brain.
2. **Indirect effect**: Acting on the brain vasomotor center, CO₂ has an extremely powerful indirect effect transmitted through the sympathetic nervous vasoconstrictor system. This causes **widespread vasoconstriction** throughout the body.

## LONG-TERM BLOOD FLOW REGULATION

### Most Vasodilators or Vasoconstrictors Have Little Effect on Long-Term Blood Flow Unless They Alter the Metabolic Rate of the Tissues

In most experimental studies, tissue blood flow and cardiac output are not substantially altered — except for 1 or 2 days — when large amounts of powerful vasoconstrictors (such as angiotensin II) or vasodilators (such as bradykinin) are chronically infused.

**Why is blood flow not significantly altered, even with large amounts of these vasoactive agents?**

To answer this question, we must return to one of the fundamental principles of circulatory function — the ability of each tissue to **autoregulate** its own blood flow according to metabolic needs.

- Administration of a powerful vasoconstrictor may cause **transient decreases** in tissue blood flow and cardiac output, but usually has **little long-term effect** if it does not alter metabolic rate.
- Likewise, most vasodilators cause only **short-term changes** if they do not alter tissue metabolism.

Therefore, blood flow is generally regulated according to the specific needs of the tissues, as long as arterial pressure is adequate to perfuse them.
