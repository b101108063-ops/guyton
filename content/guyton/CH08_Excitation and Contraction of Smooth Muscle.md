summary: "Smooth muscle contracts using actin-myosin interaction similar to skeletal muscle but lacks troponin, relying instead on Ca2+-calmodulin and myosin light chain kinase for regulation. Unitary smooth muscle forms syncytial networks with gap junctions, while multi-unit smooth muscle operates independently with discrete innervation."
key_points:
  - "Unitary (single-unit) smooth muscle cells are connected by gap junctions, allowing synchronized contraction as a functional syncytium; found in blood vessels, GI tract, uterus, and ureters"
  - "Multi-unit smooth muscle operates independently with individual nerve endings controlling each fiber; examples include the ciliary muscle, iris, and piloerector muscles"
  - "Smooth muscle lacks troponin; instead, Ca2+-calmodulin activates myosin light chain kinase (MLCK), which phosphorylates the myosin light chain to enable cross-bridge cycling"
  - "Smooth muscle can contract up to 80% of its resting length (vs. <30% for skeletal muscle) due to the side-polar arrangement of myosin cross-bridges and attachment to dense bodies"
  - "Smooth muscle contraction is typically prolonged (tonic) rather than rapid and twitch-like, making it energy-efficient for maintaining sustained tension in blood vessels and hollow organs"
