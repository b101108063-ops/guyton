Guyton & Hall 生理學 章節清單

細胞與遺傳

CH01 Functional Organization of the Human Body and Control of the Internal Environment ›
The human body consists of cells organized into tissues, organs, and organ systems that work together to maintain homeostasis.
CH02 The Cell and Its Functions ›
Cells contain specialized organelles enclosed by membranes that perform distinct functions essential for cell survival.
CH03 Genetic Control of Protein Synthesis, Cell Function, and Cell Reproduction ›
Genes control cell function through DNA transcription, RNA formation, and protein synthesis at ribosomes.
CH04 Transport of Substances Through Cell Membranes ›
Substances pass through cell membranes via diffusion, facilitated diffusion, active transport, and vesicular transport.
CH05 Membrane Potentials and Action Potentials ›
Resting membrane potential (~-70mV) and action potentials allow electrical signaling in excitable tissues.
CH06 Contraction of Skeletal Muscle ›
Skeletal muscle contraction involves neuromuscular junction, action potential propagation, and excitation-contraction coupling.
CH07 Excitation of Skeletal Muscle - Neuromuscular Transmission ›
Acetylcholine release at the neuromuscular junction triggers end-plate potentials and muscle fiber contraction.
CH08 Excitation and Contraction of Smooth Muscle ›
Smooth muscle lacks sarcomeres and is innervated by autonomic nerves with graded, slow contraction.

心臟與循環

CH09 Cardiac Muscle: The Heart as a Pump ›
The heart functions as two sequential pumps with atrial and ventricular syncytiums separated by the fibrous A-V ring.
CH10 Rhythmical Excitation of the Heart ›
The SA node initiates cardiac rhythm at 60-100 bpm, with conduction through AV node, bundle of His, and Purkinje fibers.
CH11 Fundamentals of Electrocardiography ›
ECG waveforms (P, QRS, T) reflect depolarization and repolarization of cardiac chambers.
CH12 ECG Interpretation of Cardiac Abnormalities ›
ECG detects ischemia, infarction, hypertrophy, and electrolyte disturbances affecting cardiac function.
CH13 Cardiac Arrhythmias ›
Arrhythmias result from abnormalities in impulse formation, conduction, or both, identifiable by ECG patterns.
CH14 Overview of the Circulation ›
Blood flows from high-pressure arteries through capillaries (site of exchange) to low-pressure veins.
CH15 Vascular Distensibility and Functions ›
Arterial walls distend to smooth pulsatile flow; venous distension allows large blood volume storage.
CH16 Capillary Dynamics and Fluid Exchange ›
Starling forces govern fluid movement across capillary walls: hydrostatic and oncotic pressures.
CH17 Local and Humoral Control of Blood Flow ›
Metabolic, myogenic, and endothelial mechanisms regulate tissue blood flow independently.
CH18 Nervous Regulation of Circulation ›
Sympathetic activation increases cardiac output and peripheral resistance; parasympathetic decreases heart rate.
CH19 Role of Kidneys in Long-Term Arterial Pressure ›
Kidneys regulate extracellular fluid volume, determining long-term arterial pressure via pressure-natriuresis.
CH20 Cardiac Output, Venous Return, and Regulation ›
Cardiac output equals heart rate times stroke volume, regulated by venous return and filling pressure.
CH21 Muscle Blood Flow and Cardiac Output During Exercise ›
Exercise increases muscle blood flow via metabolic vasodilation and raises cardiac output via sympathetic activation.
CH22 Cardiac Failure ›
Heart failure involves systolic (reduced ejection) or diastolic (impaired filling) dysfunction with compensatory responses.
CH23 Heart Valves and Heart Sounds ›
Valvular stenosis or regurgitation produces characteristic murmurs; congenital defects alter normal hemodynamics.
CH24 Circulatory Shock ›
Shock involves inadequate tissue perfusion: hypovolemic, cardiogenic, distributive, or obstructive causes.

腎臟與體液

CH25 Regulation of Body Fluid Compartments ›
Body water distributes between intracellular (~40% body weight) and extracellular (~20%) compartments.
CH26 The Urinary System ›
Kidneys filter blood at glomeruli, then reabsorb and secrete in tubules to form concentrated urine.
CH27 Glomerular Filtration and Renal Blood Flow ›
GFR (~180 L/day) is determined by Starling forces at the glomerular capillary.
CH28 Renal Tubular Reabsorption and Secretion ›
Most filtered solutes are reabsorbed along the nephron; selective secretion eliminates drugs and toxins.
CH29 Urine Concentration and Dilution ›
The medullary countercurrent system concentrates urine via antidiuretic hormone (ADH) action.
CH30 Renal Regulation of Electrolytes ›
Kidneys regulate potassium, calcium, phosphate, and magnesium balance through tubular transport.
CH31 Acid-Base Regulation ›
Buffers, respiration, and renal excretion of H+ maintain pH at 7.40 within narrow limits.
CH32 Diuretics and Kidney Diseases ›
Diuretics inhibit tubular reabsorption; kidney disease impairs filtration, reabsorption, and endocrine functions.

血液與免疫

CH33 Red Blood Cells, Anemia, and Polycythemia ›
RBCs transport O2 via hemoglobin; anemia reduces O2 delivery; polycythemia increases blood viscosity.
CH34 Resistance to Infection - I ›
Innate immunity (skin, phagocytes, inflammation) provides immediate defense against pathogens.
CH35 Resistance to Infection - II. Immunity and Allergy ›
Adaptive immunity involves B cells (antibodies) and T cells (cellular immunity) with antigen specificity.
CH36 Blood Types and Transfusion ›
ABO and Rh antigens determine blood compatibility for safe transfusion and transplantation.
CH37 Hemostasis and Blood Coagulation ›
Primary hemostasis (platelet plug) and secondary hemostasis (coagulation cascade) seal injured vessels.

呼吸

CH38 Pulmonary Ventilation ›
Ventilation moves air in and out of lungs via rhythmic muscle contraction; tidal volume and frequency determine alveolar ventilation.
CH39 Pulmonary Circulation ›
Low-pressure pulmonary circulation receives right ventricular output; pulmonary edema results from increased capillary pressure.
CH40 Gas Exchange ›
O2 and CO2 diffuse across the respiratory membrane according to partial pressure gradients.
CH41 Transport of O2 and CO2 ›
O2 transports bound to hemoglobin and dissolved in plasma; CO2 as bicarbonate, carbamino, and dissolved forms.
CH42 Regulation of Respiration ›
Respiratory centers in medulla and pons respond to chemical (O2, CO2, pH) and mechanical (stretch) stimuli.
CH43 Respiratory Insufficiency ›
Hypoxemia and hypercapnia result from V/Q mismatch, shunt, or hypoventilation; treated with O2 therapy.
CH44 Aviation, High Altitude, and Space Physiology ›
Altitude reduces barometric pressure and PaO2, causing hypoxia acclimatization responses.
CH45 Deep-Sea Diving Physiology ›
Hyperbaric pressure affects gas solubility; nitrogen narcosis and decompression sickness are key concerns.

神經系統

CH46 Organization of the Nervous System ›
CNS and PNS integrate sensory input and motor output via synapses using excitatory or inhibitory neurotransmitters.
CH47 Sensory Receptors ›
Receptors transduce specific stimuli into electrical signals; neuronal circuits process and integrate sensory information.
CH48 Somatic Sensations - I. Tactile and Position ›
Mechanoreceptors detect touch, pressure, vibration, and joint position via specialized nerve endings.
CH49 Somatic Sensations - II. Pain and Temperature ›
Nociceptors and thermoreceptors detect harmful stimuli and temperature changes via A-delta and C fibers.
CH53 The Sense of Hearing ›
Sound waves vibrate the tympanic membrane, ossicles amplify and transmit vibration to the cochlea.
CH54 Taste and Smell ›
Gustatory and olfactory receptors detect chemical stimuli for flavor perception and food safety.
CH55 Spinal Cord Motor Functions ›
Spinal reflexes (stretch, withdrawal) and descending pathways control motor output to skeletal muscles.
CH57 Cerebellum and Basal Ganglia ›
Cerebellum coordinates movement timing; basal ganglia initiates and regulates movement amplitude and force.
CH58 Cerebral Cortex Functions ›
Higher cortical functions include learning, memory, language, and executive control via distributed networks.
CH59 Limbic System and Hypothalamus ›
Limbic system mediates emotions; hypothalamus controls homeostasis, thirst, hunger, and autonomic functions.
CH60 States of Brain Activity ›
Sleep-wake cycles, brain waves, and pathological states (epilepsy, psychoses, dementia) reflect brain activity patterns.
CH61 Autonomic Nervous System ›
Sympathetic (fight-or-flight) and parasympathetic (rest-and-digest) systems regulate involuntary organ functions.
CH62 Cerebral Blood Flow and CSF ›
Cerebral blood flow (~750 ml/min) is tightly regulated; CSF cushions and supports the brain.

胃腸道

CH63 General Principles of GI Function ›
GI tract motility, secretion, digestion, and absorption are regulated by neural (enteric, autonomic) and hormonal mechanisms.
CH64 Propulsion and Mixing of Food ›
Peristalsis and segmentation contractions propel and mix food with digestive secretions along the alimentary tract.
CH65 Secretory Functions ›
GI secretions (saliva, gastric juice, bile, pancreatic juice, intestinal mucus) contain enzymes, electrolytes, and water.
CH66 Digestion and Absorption ›
Macronutrients are digested by enzymes and absorbed via active transport, facilitated diffusion, or passive diffusion.
CH67 Physiology of GI Disorders ›
Disorders of motility, secretion, or absorption produce syndromes such as malabsorption, diarrhea, and constipation.

代謝

CH68 Carbohydrate Metabolism ›
Glucose metabolism via glycolysis, TCA cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation yields ATP for cellular energy.
CH69 Lipid Metabolism ›
Lipids are digested, absorbed, transported as lipoproteins, and metabolized for energy or stored in adipose tissue.
CH70 Protein Metabolism ›
Amino acids from dietary protein are used for protein synthesis, gluconeogenesis, or neurotransmitter synthesis.
CH71 The Liver ›
Liver functions include metabolism, detoxification, bile secretion, synthesis of plasma proteins, and storage.
CH72 Dietary Balances and Feeding Regulation ›
Energy balance is regulated by hunger, satiety signals, and metabolic rate; obesity results from positive energy balance.
CH73 Energetics and Metabolic Rate ›
Metabolic rate (BMR, RMR) measures energy expenditure at rest or under specific conditions.
CH74 Body Temperature Regulation ›
Core temperature is maintained at ~37C via balance of heat production (muscle, liver) and loss (skin, lungs).

內分泌

CH75 Introduction to Endocrinology ›
Hormones act as chemical messengers, binding to receptors to regulate target organ functions via endocrine, paracrine, or autocrine mechanisms.
CH76 Pituitary Hormones ›
Anterior pituitary secretes GH, TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH, prolactin; posterior releases ADH and oxytocin.
CH77 Thyroid Metabolic Hormones ›
T3 and T4 regulate basal metabolic rate, growth, and development; calcitonin lowers blood calcium.
CH78 Adrenocortical Hormones ›
Cortisol (glucocorticoid), aldosterone (mineralocorticoid), and androgens are secreted by adrenal cortex.
CH79 Insulin, Glucagon, and Diabetes ›
Insulin lowers blood glucose; glucagon raises it; diabetes involves insulin deficiency or resistance.
CH80 Parathyroid Hormone and Calcium Metabolism ›
PTH raises blood calcium; calcitonin lowers it; vitamin D promotes calcium absorption.
CH81 Reproductive Functions of the Male ›
Testosterone drives male sexual development, spermatogenesis, and secondary sex characteristics.
CH82 Female Physiology ›
Estrogen and progesterone regulate menstrual cycle, ovulation, and preparation of endometrium for pregnancy.
CH83 Pregnancy and Lactation ›
Pregnancy involves hormonal maintenance, fetal development, and metabolic adaptations; lactation provides nutrition.
CH84 Fetal and Neonatal Physiology ›
Fetal gas exchange via placenta; neonatal transition involves lung aeration, circulatory reorganization, and thermoregulation.

運動生理

CH85 Sports Physiology ›
Exercise physiology covers metabolic, cardiovascular, respiratory, and muscular adaptations to physical training.
