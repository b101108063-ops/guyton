Sports Physiology

CARDIOVASCULAR RESPONSES TO EXERCISE

Acute Cardiovascular Changes

During exercise, several cardiovascular adjustments occur to meet the increased metabolic demands of working muscles:

  1. Cardiac output increases: Up to 25-35 L/min in trained athletes
  2. Heart rate increases: From resting ~70 bpm to maximal levels
  3. Stroke volume increases: Due to increased venous return and contractility
  4. Mean arterial pressure increases: Due to increased cardiac output and peripheral resistance
  5. Blood flow redistributes: From splanchnic organs to skeletal muscles

VO2 Max

VO2 max is the gold standard measure of aerobic capacity. It represents the maximum rate at which oxygen can be consumed during intense exercise.

VO2 max = Cardiac Output × Arteriovenous O2 difference

Parameter Adaptation
Resting heart rate Decreased

Stroke volume Increased

RESPIRATORY RESPONSES TO EXERCISE

Acute Respiratory Changes

During exercise, respiratory adjustments include:

  1. Ventilation increases: Linear increase with work intensity, then exponential at high intensities
  2. Tidal volume increases: From ~500 mL at rest to 2-3 L
  3. Respiratory rate increases: From ~12-15/min to 40-50/min
  4. Oxygen extraction increases: A-vO2 difference widens

Respiratory Adaptations to Training

Parameter Adaptation
Vital capacity Slightly increased
Residual volume No change or slightly decreased
Diffusing capacity Increased
Ventilation at given work rate More efficient (lower V̇E/V̇O2)

Oxygen-Hemoglobin Dissociation Curve During Exercise

The curve shifts to the right during exercise due to:

  • Increased temperature
  • Increased CO2
  • Increased H+ (decreased pH)
  • Increased 2,3-diphosphoglycerate (2,3-DPG)

This shift enhances O2 delivery to working muscles.

Fatigue Mechanisms

Fatigue during exercise results from multiple factors:

  1. Phosphate (Pi) accumulation: Inhibits cross-bridge cycling
  2. H+ (acidosis): Impairs contractile proteins and enzymes
  3. Ammonia accumulation: From amino acid catabolism
  4. Glycogen depletion: Especially in Type II fibers
  5. Central nervous system fatigue: Decreased motor cortex drive

METABOLIC RESPONSES TO EXERCISE

Carbohydrate Metabolism During Exercise

  1. Muscle glycogenolysis: Primary source for high-intensity exercise
  2. Gluconeogenesis: Liver glycogen supports blood glucose
  3. Glycogen supercompensation: Enhanced storage after depletion (carb loading)

Fat Metabolism During Exercise

  1. Lipolysis: Increased adipose tissue breakdown
  2. Fatty acid oxidation: Primary fuel during low-intensity, prolonged exercise
  3. Beta-oxidation: Mitochondrial energy production from fats

Protein Metabolism During Exercise

  1. Amino acid oxidation: Provides energy, especially during prolonged exercise
  2. Alanine cycle: Glucose-alanine cycle between muscle and liver
  3. Ammonia production: From amino acid deamination

ENVIRONMENTAL FACTORS IN SPORTS

Heat and Exercise

Condition Effects Management
Hot environment Increased sweating, cardiovascular strain Acclimatization, hydration
Cold environment Shivering, increased VO2 cost Layering, pre-warming
Altitude Decreased PO2, reduced VO2 max Acclimatization, altitude training
Humidity Impaired cooling Proper hydration, cooling strategies

Altitude Effects on Performance

At altitude (above 5,000 ft / 1,500 m):

  1. Barometric pressure decreases
  2. PO2 decreases
  3. VO2 max decreases (5-7% per 1,000 m above 5,000 ft)
  4. Anaerobic threshold decreases
  5. Respiratory rate increases

Acclimatization to Altitude

Duration Adaptations
Hours Hyperventilation, HR increase
Days Increased RBC production (EPO)
Weeks Further hematologic adaptations, improved efficiency
Months Near-complete normalization of VO2 max
